# An Interactive Dastan game made on Twine

An interactive fiction game built in Twine (SugarCube), created as a course project for **Codex to Hypertext**. It's an adaptation of *Dastan-e-Chouboli*, a Rajasthani folk tale written by Vijaydan Detha and popularized through the oral storytelling art of Dastangoi.

**Play it here:** [dexter-smash.itch.io/group-3-twine](https://dexter-smash.itch.io/group-3-twine)

## Design Highlights

- **Point-and-click storytelling** — A minigame is embedded directly into the narrative for the "Who will give the Hunkara" section, inspired by classic point-and-click adventure games.
- **Visual novel-style UI** — Characters appear on screen against backgrounds, with dialogue delivered through a text box, for visual clarity and easy interaction.
- **Escalating tension through UI** — As the story builds toward Chouboli's breaking point, buttons grow progressively larger and colors intensify, visually "rage-baiting" the player into speaking the truth — mirroring how the storyteller rage-baits Chouboli in the tale.
- **Branching paths with memory** — Variables (like whether the player encounters "the thief" or chooses to "stay") track earlier choices and change later dialogue accordingly, so decisions feel consequential without needing infinite paths.
- **Illusion of free choice** — Branching is designed so player agency feels real while still guiding the story toward the same thematic destination: truth always comes out.

## Performance Elements

1. **Video Narrations** — Seven short (15–30 second) voiceover sequences play at scene transitions, echoing the atmospheric scene-setting of traditional Dastangoi performers. These separate world-building (video) from player choice (text) to keep the pacing intact.
2. **Adaptive Background Music** — Music shifts with context, much like live musicians accompanied traditional dastangoi performances. Music fades out during internal-struggle sequences, making the escalating UI (darkening backgrounds and growing buttons) feel more oppressive.


## Possible Issues
- If you get stuck at any point, **click the necklace** to progress — this is a required interaction that isn't always obvious.


## Learning Outcome
This was our first time working with Twine/SugarCube, Git/GitHub, itch.io, and VS Code together as a pipeline. Most of what we learned about SugarCube came from studying existing itch.io games and diving into the documentation. The biggest narrative takeaway: players don't need infinite paths, they need their choices to feel consequential in the moment, it is just the illusion of free will.
